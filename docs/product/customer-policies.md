# Customer policies and commitments

**Status:** Product policy baseline; publication copy requires final legal/operational review  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-09-29  
**Customer-safe:** No until final publication review; approved facts are copied into the support knowledge base  
**Jurisdiction baseline:** UK consumer sales

This document converts the strategy and `policies/policies.ods` into one policy baseline. It does not remove or limit statutory rights.

## Prices, postage, and delivery insurance

- Displayed order prices include standard outbound postage and any delivery insurance FlipFlop chooses to purchase. These are free of an additional charge to the customer, but their cost is included in the commercial price model.
- Faster or customer-requested premium delivery may carry a clearly disclosed additional charge.
- FlipFlop remains responsible for the goods until delivered to the customer or the customer's nominated recipient, subject to applicable law.
- Delivery estimates use current supplier, workshop, and courier evidence. A date is a guarantee only when the checkout explicitly says so.

## Change-of-mind returns

For online consumer orders, the baseline FlipFlop promise is:

- notify FlipFlop within 14 days after delivery that the customer wishes to cancel;
- return the goods within a further 14 days;
- refund within 14 days after the goods are returned or the customer provides acceptable evidence of return;
- refund standard outbound delivery where it was separately charged;
- the customer normally pays change-of-mind return postage unless the order terms say otherwise;
- a deduction may be made for handling beyond what would reasonably be permitted in a shop where the law allows.

Although genuinely bespoke goods can sometimes be exempt from distance-selling cancellation, FlipFlop should not rely on that exemption in customer copy without a reviewed, product-specific legal basis. Faulty, damaged, misdescribed, or incorrectly supplied goods follow statutory remedies rather than the change-of-mind process.

## Faults and statutory rights

Goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, and as described. The policy flow must support:

- the short-term right to reject qualifying faulty goods within 30 days;
- repair or replacement where required after that period;
- the six-month presumption applicable to faults, unless FlipFlop can establish the legal exception;
- price reduction or final rejection where repair/replacement fails or is not provided within a reasonable time without significant inconvenience;
- reasonable necessary return costs, including postage, for faulty, damaged, or misdescribed goods.

The customer may have rights for up to six years in England and Wales and five years in Scotland. That does not mean every product must last that long or that every later problem is an original fault.

## FlipFlop commercial warranty

- New complete FlipFlop builds include a 12-month FlipFlop warranty beginning on recorded customer delivery.
- The warranty is additional to statutory rights and does not replace or shorten them.
- Warranty coverage follows the complete-build terms supplied with the order, including parts and labour where stated.
- Customer-caused damage, misuse, unauthorised modifications, fair wear, and disclosed pre-existing limitations may be excluded only where lawful and clearly relevant.
- Flip Refresh work warrants the supplied parts and FlipFlop workmanship for the stated period. It does not turn every retained customer-owned component into a newly warranted component and does not remove statutory service rights.

## Cancellations and changes before build work

- Before components are ordered, direct customers may cancel through self-service for an automatic full refund.
- Before components are ordered, a change request pauses sourcing and is reviewed for compatibility, timing, and price before acceptance.
- After components are ordered, self-service cancellation/change closes and the customer contacts support. FlipFlop assesses supplier commitments, work already performed, contract terms, and statutory rights.

## Delivery damage and insurance claims

Customers should photograph visible package damage before opening where practical, retain all packaging, document product damage, and contact FlipFlop promptly. Operationally, FlipFlop should preserve:

- working-build evidence;
- package contents before packing;
- the sealed parcel and label;
- value and proof-of-sale evidence;
- courier service and signed-for evidence;
- tracking events and delivery evidence;
- customer photos and carrier reservations/incident references.

The selected Figural/Secursus process requires tracking, adult/direct signature, new resistant double packaging in a rigid outer box, discreet packaging, and photos of the parcel and its contents before shipment. Parcel shops/lockers require written approval, and computer screens require insurer packaging validation. Warranty-repair return shipments are not automatically covered by the original outbound policy; confirm separate cover with the insurer. The current workflow also preserves working-build media and records the operator's packing confirmations. Insurance must be active before handover and no later than the shipment day. The provider's UK terms give short claim deadlines; support must escalate potential claims immediately and follow the active policy terms. Insurance terms govern the insurer relationship; they do not reduce the customer's rights against FlipFlop.

For insured loss or damage, customers should notify FlipFlop immediately, retain all packaging, and use the private case portal to provide relevant photos, the shipping label, proof of value, carrier claim/incident record, delivery damage notation or non-delivery evidence, and a police reference where theft is involved. The current Secursus UK guide says to report to the carrier immediately and file with Secursus within 14 days; staff must verify the active policy terms and escalate promptly.
