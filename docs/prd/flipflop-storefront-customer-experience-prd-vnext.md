# FlipFlop Storefront & Customer Experience --- PRD vNext

**Status:** Proposed customer-experience update\
**Site:** theflipflop.shop\
**Brand:** *Beautiful machines, built to be admired.*\
**Functional promise:** **The right PC. Without needing to become a PC
expert.**

This PRD consumes the Playbook, Recommendation, Procurement, Pricing,
Ready-to-Ship/Gem, Order and Upgrade services defined by the Core
Commerce & Intelligence PRD.

## 1. Experience vision

Conventional PC shops force customers to shop in industry language: CPU
model numbers, chipsets, RAM timings and hundreds of near-identical
products.

FlipFlop reverses that relationship.

The customer says: \> "I want to play at 1440p."\
\> "I write software and use Docker."\
\> "I want local AI."\
\> "My son's PC is getting slow."\
\> "I have about £1,100."

FlipFlop translates the need into technical decisions, explains the
important choices and builds a machine that feels personal.

Desired emotional sequence:

**Curiosity → relief → understanding → trust → excitement → ownership**

The storefront is therefore a **guided expert conversation inside a
cinematic premium retail experience**, not primarily a catalogue.

# 2. The five FlipFlop promises

These are product behaviours as well as marketing messages.

## 1 --- We choose the complicated stuff for you

**Message:** *Tell us what you want your PC to do. We'll handle the
component homework.*

Proof: use-case-first journey, few meaningful questions, a
recommendation instead of a SKU wall, optional technical detail.

## 2 --- Your PC doesn't have to look like everyone else's

**Message:** *Performance chosen for you. Style chosen by you.*

Proof: compatible case choices, colour/lighting themes, clean vs
showcase styles, build identity/name and optional personalised
treatment.

## 3 --- Custom built doesn't have to mean a long wait

**Message:** *Choose whether speed or maximum sourcing flexibility
matters more to you.*

Proof: Priority, Standard, Flexible and Ready-to-Ship matches, with
honest eligibility.

## 4 --- We'll show you where your money is going

**Message:** *No mystery box. We'll explain the choices that matter and
why.*

Proof: exact final specification, "why we chose this", budget
allocation, condition disclosure and no silent downgrades.

## 5 --- We won't encourage you to waste money

**Message:** *If an upgrade won't meaningfully improve what you do,
we'll tell you.*

Proof: anti-upsell warnings, Save option, upgrade-vs-replace advice and
practical-benefit explanations.

# 5. Find My PC interaction rules

-   plain English first;
-   approximately one conceptual decision per screen;
-   visible progress;
-   "Why are we asking?";
-   "I'm not sure" is always acceptable where sensible;
-   free-text "tell us what you need" route;
-   skip irrelevant questions dynamically;
-   never require component knowledge;
-   preserve answers when navigating back;
-   target 2--4 minutes for mainstream users.

# 6. Questions to ask

## A. Primary use

**What will you mainly use your PC for?** Gaming / Software development
/ AI-local models / Content creation / Study / Business-office /
Family-home / A mixture.

Allow secondary uses.

## B. Budget

**What would you like to spend?**\
Ranges + custom amount.

Then: **Is that a firm ceiling, or may we show a slightly more expensive
option when the improvement is genuinely worthwhile?** - Firm maximum -
Small stretch is OK - Show me the best-value point

"Stretch" is not permission to automatically consume the budget.

## C. Workload questions

**Gaming:** games, resolution, refresh rate ("I don't know" allowed),
competitive FPS vs visual quality, ray tracing importance,
streaming/recording.

**Software development:** languages/toolchains, Docker, VMs, local DBs,
compilation, monitors, local AI coding.

**AI:** local workloads/model sizes where known, image/video generation,
experimentation vs sustained work, multi-GPU aspirations, power/noise.

**Content creation:** applications, resolution/codecs,
photo/video/3D/audio, project sizes, render priority.

Other Playbooks add equivalent targeted questions.

## D. Practical needs

Only ask when relevant: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, storage, small-case
requirement, noise, monitor/peripherals, reusable parts,
accessibility/practical constraints, desired useful life.

## E. Appearance

**What should your PC feel like?** - Clean & minimal - Showcase RGB -
Dark / stealth - Light / white - FlipFlop orange + blue - Surprise me

Only show physically compatible cases/styles.

## F. Condition/value preference

Default: **new components**.

For Flexible: \> **Want us to look even harder for value?** We can
optionally include approved refurbished or used components. We'll show
condition and warranty implications before you agree.

Choices: - New only - New + approved refurbished - Include approved used
opportunities

This choice is binding.

# 7. Recommendation reveal

This is the centrepiece.

## Hero recommendation

> **We think this is your sweet spot.**

Show build identity/name, price, outcome-oriented capability summary,
why it fits, visual and delivery choices.

Do **not** lead with a BOM.

## Mandatory: "Why we've spent your money this way"

Explain component allocation in the customer's language.

Example: **Graphics --- where we're investing most**\
Your stated games/resolution benefit more from a stronger GPU, so this
is where we've concentrated the budget.

**Processor --- enough headroom without overspending**\
The next tier would cost more but is not the best use of your budget for
the workload you described.

Then memory, storage and other material decisions.

## Technical drawer

CTA: **Show me the nerdy stuff**

Reveal exact components, technical details, compatibility and benchmark
evidence. Experts get depth without forcing it on everyone.

# 8. Save / Recommended / Stretch

Show up to three only when useful:

-   **SAVE:** cheapest solution meeting hard requirements.
-   **RECOMMENDED:** best Playbook balance.
-   **STRETCH:** more expensive only when benefit is material.

Describe differences as outcomes, not model-number trivia.

Never use "Most Popular" without real data.

# 9. Delivery choice

Show this **after** the customer understands the recommended machine.

## ⚡ Priority

**3 working days · +£49 when eligible**\
\> We restrict sourcing to our rapid-delivery network and prioritise
your build in the workshop.

Supplier names remain internal. Core uses Amazon + Overclockers.

## 📦 Standard

**5 working days when eligible**\
\> We use our approved retail network to balance speed and value.

## 💰 Flexible

**Best sourcing flexibility**\
\> Give us longer to hunt for the best compliant parts. Follow every
stage in My FlipFlop while we keep your estimated delivery range
updated.

Where known, show real saving: \> **Save £86 by choosing Flexible**

Never promise a fixed delivery date. Show current estimate/range,
sourcing policy, maximum sourcing window and what happens if sourcing
fails.

## 🚀 Ready-to-Ship Match

> **We already have a FlipFlop that fits your needs.**

Show next-working-day dispatch eligibility, exact
condition/specification and why it matches.

# 10. Make It Yours

Personalisation happens after performance is secured: - case; -
lighting/theme; - colour; - clean/minimal; - build name; - personalised
plaque/identity; - sensible RAM/storage/performance options; - selected
accessories.

For every performance upgrade show cost and likely benefit **for this
customer**.

Where poor value: \> **We'd save your money here.**

# 11. Payment & finance experience

Show full cash price prominently. Where a connected provider supports
it, show compliant indicative instalment messaging, e.g. eligible
options/from £X per month, with terms/eligibility disclosure.

Checkout can support cards/wallets plus Klarna-style BNPL/instalment or
other eligible UK finance providers through the Core provider
abstraction.

Never imply guaranteed credit or that FlipFlop is the lender when it is
not.

Before payment show: - selected recommendation; - exact/committed
specification policy; - component condition policy; - fulfilment mode; -
price; - timing/estimate; - personalisation; - warranty/support; -
substitution policy; - cancellation/returns; - payment method.

Procurement begins only after safe payment status.

# 12. Upgrade My PC

Make this a primary service, not a footer extra.

## Proposition

> # Love your PC again.
>
> You may not need a new computer. Send us the one you have and we'll
> tell you what is worth keeping, what is holding it back, and what we'd
> change.

This is powerful proof of Promise 5.

## Assessment

Ask for: - model/specification; - photos; - optional system-report
upload; - what feels limiting; - desired workloads; - budget; -
aesthetic goals; - noise/storage goals; - turnaround need.

Recommendation: - **KEEP** - **UPGRADE** - **OPTIONAL** - **DON'T SPEND
HERE** - **TRANSFORM**

Transform can include case transplant, cooling, cabling, cleaning and
lighting.

## Fulfilment

**Standard Upgrade** and **Fast Track Upgrade**. Fast Track is shown
only when workshop capacity and parts support it.

After receipt, physically validate the submitted specification. Any
material price/scope change requires explicit customer approval.

Return package includes final specification, work performed, relevant
before/after benchmarks/temperatures, replaced-parts disposition, photos
and future upgrade path.

# 13. My FlipFlop portal

Made-to-order status: **Order confirmed → Finding/ordering parts → Parts
on their way → Parts arrived → Building → Testing → Ready → On its way**

Flexible additionally shows: - parts secured/outstanding; - latest
sourcing event; - current estimated delivery range; - confidence; -
explanation of changes; - approvals needed.

Upgrade status: **Return/shipping → Received → Inspection →
Recommendation confirmed → Parts → Upgrade/transformation → QA → Return
shipment**

Future portal: component inventory, warranty, benchmark report,
manuals/downloads, support and future upgrade path.

The portal should make watching the machine come to life enjoyable
without inventing progress.

# 14. Ready-to-Ship

Position as: \> **One-of-one machines, already built.**

Each page shows exact spec, condition, story/build identity, benchmark
evidence, detailed photography, suitable Playbooks, price, dispatch
eligibility and warranty/support.

These machines also surface automatically inside Find My PC when they
match.

# 15. Cinematic design system

-   premium dark presentation;
-   FlipFlop orange/blue used deliberately;
-   NASA-inspired headings consistent with existing brand assets;
-   real hardware photography;
-   depth/parallax where useful;
-   restrained glow;
-   crisp technical overlays;
-   avoid generic "RGB gamer soup."

Signature interactions may include: - PC adapting visually to selected
use/style; - budget allocation animating across major components; -
delivery mode changing a sourcing-network/timeline visual; - live Make
It Yours case/lighting preview; - Upgrade before→after transformation; -
My FlipFlop build timeline.

Respect reduced motion. Core recommendation/checkout must remain usable
without WebGL/heavy animation.

# 16. Trust architecture

Required: - exact parts or clearly defined substitution rules; -
explicit condition; - "why chosen" explanations; - realistic timing; -
warranty/support visibility; - benchmark methodology; - customer
approval for material substitutions; - transparent finance messaging; -
no fake scarcity/countdowns; - no invented crossed-out RRPs; - no
upgrade recommendation without stated benefit.

# 17. Tone

Expert, warm, plain-English, enthusiastic and never condescending.

Prefer: \> "You don't need the more expensive CPU for what you've told
us. We'd put that £120 into the graphics card instead."

Avoid empty superlatives and jargon-first selling.

Track questionnaire start/completion/abandonment, "I'm not sure",
recommendation acceptance, Save/Recommended/Stretch, technical drawer,

# 20. Accessibility/performance

WCAG-aligned interaction, keyboard operation, semantic forms, reduced
motion, contrast, responsive/mobile flow, lazy heavy assets and graceful
fallback. Cinematic design must never break commerce.

# 21. Acceptance criteria

The storefront is acceptable when: 1. a non-technical customer can reach
a defensible recommendation without choosing components; 2. the site
explains why it fits; 3. every recommendation explains where the money
is being invested; 4. poor-value upgrades trigger honest advice; 5.
Priority/Standard/Flexible are customer-benefit labels, not supplier
labels; 6. Priority appears only when Core confirms eligibility; 7.
Flexible never makes a false fixed-date promise; 8. Flexible exposes
sourcing progress and an updated ETA/range; 9. non-new sourcing requires
explicit consent; 10. Ready-to-Ship can appear inside normal
recommendations; 11. finance messaging never implies guaranteed credit;
12. exact condition/timing/price/substitution policy are visible before
payment; 13. Upgrade My PC can advise against unnecessary work; 14.
Upgrade Fast Track is capacity/parts gated; 15. My FlipFlop exposes real
progress; 16. the five promises are evidenced throughout; 17. cinematic
visuals degrade gracefully; 18. expert technical detail remains
available without cluttering the default path.

# 22. North-star journey

A customer arrives knowing only:

> "I have about £1,100 and want a great gaming PC."

They leave knowing: - what FlipFlop recommends; - what it will do; - why
money is allocated that way; - what they could save; - what spending
more would actually achieve; - how speed changes price; - whether
patience can save money; - whether a Ready-to-Ship machine already
fits; - how to personalise it; - how they can pay; - and that FlipFlop
would rather tell them **not to spend £150 unnecessarily** than take
£150 they do not need to spend.

That trust is the storefront's principal product advantage.
