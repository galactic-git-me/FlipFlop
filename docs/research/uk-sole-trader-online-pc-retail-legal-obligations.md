# UK sole trader online PC retail: legal and regulatory obligations

**Research date:** 29 September 2026  
**Scope:** A UK sole trader selling new, refurbished and custom-built PCs to consumers through a direct storefront and marketplaces, and supplying optional software and AI setup services. The product-safety sections distinguish Great Britain from Northern Ireland where relevant.

This is an implementation-oriented review of primary official sources, not legal advice. Requirements can depend on how FlipFlop sources, imports, brands and materially modifies equipment. The areas marked **legal review** should be checked with Trading Standards, an appropriately qualified product-compliance adviser or a solicitor before launch.

## Executive conclusion

FlipFlop does **not** need a Companies House number or registered office statement while it is genuinely operated as a sole trader. It must, however, identify the proprietor, trading name and a real geographic/service address. An email-only contact page is insufficient.

The storefront should have, and make persistently accessible, these customer-facing pages:

1. **Terms of Sale**, including identity, contract formation, pricing, payment, custom-build approval, delivery, cancellation, faults and statutory rights.
2. **Delivery Policy**, including delivery options, costs, estimates, tracking, risk and failed delivery.
3. **Returns and Cancellations Policy**, including the model cancellation form and separate rules for change-of-mind cancellation, faulty goods and services.
4. **Warranty and Repairs Policy**, describing the 12-month commercial warranty without limiting statutory rights.
5. **Privacy Notice**, covering direct and marketplace customers, accounts, build portals, payments, couriers, insurers, LLM/AI providers, fraud, marketing and retention.
6. **Cookie Policy and consent controls** for every non-essential cookie or similar technology.
7. **WEEE and Recycling page**, explaining like-for-like takeback or the Distributor Takeback Scheme route actually used.
8. **Complaints page**, with the route to any ADR scheme FlipFlop is required or contractually committed to use.
9. **Accessibility statement** as strong recommended practice and evidence of reasonable accessibility work.
10. **Business/contact disclosure** in the footer and/or legal/contact page: proprietor's legal name, `trading as FlipFlop`, geographic address, email and effective direct-contact route, plus VAT number if registered.

A modern slavery statement is **not mandatory** unless annual turnover reaches £36 million. Publishing a short voluntary ethical-sourcing statement is permissible, but it must be accurate and should not imply statutory coverage.

## Status key

- **Mandatory** — a legal duty likely to apply to this business model.
- **Conditional** — mandatory only if the stated trigger applies.
- **Recommended** — not necessarily a standalone page requirement, but prudent for transparency, accessibility, evidence or support.
- **Not applicable** — based on the stated sole-trader model and assumed scale.
- **Legal review** — application depends on facts or a legally sensitive classification.

## 1. Trader identity and website disclosures

| Status | Obligation | Practical implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **Mandatory** | An online service provider must make its name, geographic establishment address and details including email available in a form that is easy, direct and permanently accessible. | Put the proprietor's legal name, `trading as FlipFlop`, geographic address and email/direct contact route in a persistent footer-linked Legal & Contact page. Do not use only a contact form or PO Box. |
| **Mandatory** | Before a distance order, provide the trader's identity, business address, rapid contact details and a complaints address if different. | Surface this in Terms of Sale and during checkout, before the order button. Repeat it in the durable order-confirmation email. |
| **Mandatory** | A sole trader using a business name must show their own name and business name on official paperwork; invoices need an address where legal documents can be served. | Invoice as `FULL LEGAL NAME trading as FlipFlop`, with the service address and all normal invoice fields. |
| **Conditional** | Display a VAT registration number where registered, and issue VAT-compliant invoices when required. | Add the VAT number only after registration. Prices offered to consumers must include VAT and unavoidable charges. |
| **Not applicable** | Company registration number, registered office and Companies House registered-name disclosures. | Do not invent a company number or call the address a registered office while trading as an individual. These rules apply to incorporated companies. |

Sources: [Electronic Commerce Regulations 2002, regulation 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/2013/regulation/6), [Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, Schedule 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/schedule/2), [GOV.UK sole-trader naming](https://www.gov.uk/become-sole-trader/choose-your-business-name), and [GOV.UK invoice requirements](https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers/invoices-what-they-must-include).

**Privacy/address warning:** using a home address can make it public. A legitimate service/business address may be used where it accurately satisfies the rules and legal documents can be received. Obtain advice before hiding the only real establishment address.

## 2. Ecommerce information, checkout and contract formation

### Mandatory pre-contract information

Before the customer orders, provide clearly and intelligibly:

- the main characteristics and condition of the PC, including whether components are new, used or refurbished;
- the total price including tax and every unavoidable fee, or a sufficiently clear calculation method where a variable mandatory charge genuinely cannot be calculated yet;
- separately identified optional delivery/postage charges;
- payment methods, delivery arrangements, costs and expected timing;
- cancellation rights, return-cost responsibility and a model cancellation form;
- complaint handling, after-sales service and commercial warranty conditions;
- contract duration/termination where relevant;
- deposits or other financial guarantees;
- for digital content, functionality, compatibility and relevant technical protection measures; and
- any service or digital-content early-performance consent and its consequences.

Directly before submission, the important characteristics, total price and any contractual duration must be prominent. The final button must unambiguously communicate an obligation to pay, such as **Pay now** or **Place order and pay**. Optional extras must use affirmative selection rather than pre-ticked boxes. Supply the concluded contract on a durable medium, normally email, no later than delivery and before service performance where required.

Sources: [GOV.UK distance-selling duties](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses), [Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134), and [government implementation guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/429300/bis-13-1368-consumer-contracts-information-cancellation-and-additional-payments-regulations-guidance.pdf).

### Price presentation

**Mandatory:** show the total price upfront wherever a product and price amount to an invitation to purchase. This now includes mandatory fees, taxes and charges. Hiding compulsory delivery, preparation, insurance or setup charges until checkout is prohibited drip pricing. If a mandatory variable price cannot reasonably be known, show a calculation method with the same prominence as the headline price. Optional upgrades must be described and priced as optional.

This means FlipFlop should include free postage and delivery insurance in the displayed product total if every customer must pay for them through the build price. It should not present them as free if their cost is later added as a mandatory checkout fee.

Sources: [CMA price-transparency summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209/providing-clear-and-accurate-information-about-prices-summary) and [CMA unfair-commercial-practices guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices).

### Marketplace sales

**Mandatory:** consumer law follows the trader's sale even where eBay or another marketplace supplies the interface. Marketplace terms do not remove FlipFlop's duties for product description, conformity, refunds, delivery, warranty or safety. Ensure each listing contains the material product/price/condition information that the channel supports, and supply missing terms and cancellation information on a durable medium after purchase. Do not divert customers from a marketplace in breach of channel rules.

**Conditional:** a platform may collect and report the sole trader's identity, address, date of birth, tax identification number and income to HMRC. This is a platform/tax-reporting obligation rather than a public website disclosure. Source: [GOV.UK digital-platform seller reporting](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/selling-goods-or-services-on-a-digital-platform).

## 3. Cancellation, returns, faults and delivery

### Change of mind

**Mandatory baseline:** for ordinary distance-sale goods, the consumer normally has 14 days from physical receipt to notify cancellation, then another 14 days to return the goods. Refund within 14 days after receipt of the returned goods or adequate proof of return. Refund standard outbound delivery; any premium over standard delivery need not be refunded. A deduction may be made for value diminished by handling beyond what would be allowed in a shop. State before purchase if the consumer must pay return costs; for goods not normally returnable by post, give a reasonable return-cost estimate.

If the required cancellation information is omitted, cancellation rights can extend by up to 12 months. Provide the statutory model cancellation form even if an easier portal cancellation route is also offered.

Sources: [GOV.UK returns and refunds](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds) and [Consumer Contracts Regulations, regulations 29–35 and Schedule 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134).

### Custom-built and personalised PCs — **legal review before relying on exemption**

The regulations exclude goods made to the consumer's specifications or clearly personalised from the distance-sale cancellation right. A genuinely one-off Made-by-you PC may qualify. A curated build assembled from standard catalogue choices may not automatically qualify merely because it is built after ordering. Do not apply a blanket “custom builds cannot be cancelled or returned” term without obtaining advice on each workflow.

Even where the change-of-mind exemption applies, it cannot remove remedies for faulty, unsafe, misdescribed or unfit goods.

Source: [Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, regulation 28(1)(b)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/28).

### Services and digital content

Software installation, AI setup, overhaul work and similar labour are services; downloadable/setup artifacts may also be digital content. If performance begins during the 14-day service cancellation period, collect the customer's **express request** to start early and explain the proportionate charge that may become due on cancellation. The service right is lost only when the service is fully performed after the required request/acknowledgement. Digital content supplied without a tangible medium requires express consent to start and acknowledgement that cancellation rights will be lost.

Use separate unchecked acknowledgements; do not bury them in the general terms. Source: [Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, regulations 36–37](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134).

### Faulty goods and statutory rights

**Mandatory:** goods must be as described, of satisfactory quality and fit for purpose. Services must be performed with reasonable care and skill. Commercial policies cannot replace or restrict these rights. The Consumer Rights Act includes the short-term right to reject within 30 days, then repair/replacement routes and, where conditions are met, price reduction/final rejection. A fault arising in the first six months is generally treated as having existed at delivery unless the trader proves otherwise or that presumption is incompatible with the circumstances.

Sources: [Consumer Rights Act 2015, Part 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/part/1) and [GOV.UK implied/statutory rights](https://www.gov.uk/unfair-terms-in-sales-contracts/implied-rights).

### Delivery and transit risk

**Mandatory:** deliver without undue delay and normally within 30 days unless another period has been agreed. The trader generally bears risk until the consumer, or their nominated recipient, physically receives the goods when the trader arranges the carrier. Courier insurance is FlipFlop's protection; it must not be used to tell consumers that their statutory remedy depends on the insurer accepting a claim.

Source: [Consumer Rights Act 2015, sections 28–29](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/part/1/chapter/2/crossheading/other-rules-about-contracts).

## 4. Warranty, repairs and “Flip Refresh” overhaul service

| Status | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Mandatory** | A free commercial guarantee must be written in plain language, state its coverage and essential claim process, and say that statutory rights are unaffected. Its advertising and promise become contractually binding. |
| **Mandatory** | Distinguish the 12-month FlipFlop warranty from manufacturer warranties and statutory remedies. Never say rights expire after 12 months. |
| **Mandatory** | For customer-owned-PC overhaul work, perform services with reasonable care and skill and make new/replacement parts conform to contract. A statement that “only upgraded parts are warranted” cannot waive liability for damage caused to the customer's PC or statutory service remedies. |
| **Recommended** | Record pre-existing condition, customer data/back-up choices, agreed work, replaced components, test results and custody/shipping condition. Use a separate repair/overhaul contract and approval record. |

Source: [Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 50 and sections 54–57](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/part/1/chapter/4) and [section 30 commercial guarantees](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/30).

## 5. Privacy, customer/build portals and AI processing

### Privacy notice — mandatory

Provide privacy information when collecting data and, for indirect marketplace orders, within the applicable time limit and no later than first communication/disclosure where required. It should identify:

- the sole trader as controller and provide contact details;
- each purpose and lawful basis;
- legitimate interests relied upon;
- categories of data received indirectly and their source, including marketplaces;
- recipients/categories: payment providers, marketplaces, couriers, delivery insurer, email provider, hosting, support, analytics, LLM/AI providers and professional advisers;
- international transfers and safeguards;
- retention periods or criteria;
- rights, withdrawal of consent, objection and ICO complaint route;
- contractual/statutory data requirements and consequences of withholding it;
- automated decision-making/profiling information where applicable.

The notice must cover the customer account and multiple build portals, order/address snapshots, support conversations, performance/benchmark data, courier events, insurance evidence and claims, warranty administration, uploaded media and any AI-personalisation inputs.

Source: [ICO required privacy information](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/getting-started-with-gdpr/data-protection-self-assessment/what-information-you-must-supply-under-the-gdpr/).

### AI and LLM services — conditional and legal review

- Do not send customer personal data, address/order data, support transcripts or uploaded evidence to an LLM merely because the privacy notice mentions AI. Establish necessity, lawful basis, minimisation, processor terms, retention/training controls and any international-transfer mechanism.
- Tell customers when an AI-generated recommendation materially shapes a purchasing choice; ensure claims remain truthful and allow human review/escalation.
- A local LLM does not remove privacy/security obligations if it processes identifiable customer data.
- Treat software/AI account credentials and build setup artifacts as security-sensitive. Do not collect customer passwords unnecessarily or print secrets into downloadable ZIPs/logs.

### ICO fee — conditional

Most data controllers must assess whether they owe the annual ICO data-protection fee; the ICO expressly includes sole traders. Complete the official self-assessment and retain its result. Source: [ICO registration and fee](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/register/).

## 6. Cookies and electronic marketing

### Cookies and similar technologies

**Mandatory:** tell users what storage/access technologies do and why, and obtain valid consent before setting non-essential cookies, pixels, local-storage identifiers, fingerprinting or equivalent technologies. Strictly necessary basket, login/security and requested-service technologies can be exempt from consent, but should still be explained. Reject must be as accessible as accept; continuing to browse is not consent. Keep a current cookie inventory and allow withdrawal/change of choice.

Sources: [ICO cookies guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/cookies-and-similar-technologies/) and [strictly necessary exception](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/what-are-the-exceptions/).

### Marketing email/text

**Mandatory:** service messages about an order, safety issue, dispatch or warranty are not marketing if they remain operational. Promotional email/text to individuals normally needs consent unless the PECR soft opt-in applies to an existing customer for FlipFlop's similar products/services, details were obtained during a sale/negotiation, and an opt-out was offered at collection and in every message. Identify the sender and provide a valid unsubscribe address. Never make marketing consent a condition of buying.

Marketplace-provided contact data must not be reused for off-platform marketing unless both the law and marketplace terms allow it.

Sources: [ICO electronic marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/) and [ICO direct-marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/).

## 7. Product safety, electrical conformity and traceability

### Custom-built PCs

**Mandatory/conditional:** when FlipFlop assembles and markets a finished PC under its own name, it is likely to be the manufacturer of the finished electrical equipment for applicable product rules. The GB manufacturer has ultimate responsibility for the compliant finished product even where components are already conformity marked. Duties can include identifying applicable legislation/standards, risk and conformity assessment, technical documentation, declaration of conformity, appropriate UKCA or recognised CE marking, manufacturer identity/address, instructions/safety information and records.

Source: [OPSS UKCA/CE conformity guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/ukca-marking-conformity-assessment-and-documentation) and [Electrical Equipment (Safety) Regulations GB guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-equipment-safety-regulations-2016/electrical-equipment-safety-regulations-2016-great-britain).

**Legal review:** obtain a product-compliance assessment for the exact PC system, including Electrical Equipment (Safety), electromagnetic compatibility, RoHS and radio-equipment implications where Wi-Fi/Bluetooth are integrated. Northern Ireland has a distinct regime. Do not assume that conformity marks on the PSU, motherboard and other parts automatically certify the assembled branded PC.

### Refurbished and upgraded equipment

Repaired/refurbished equipment that does not change original performance, purpose or type is not treated as new under the electrical-equipment guidance and does not need recertification/re-marking on that basis. Material changes or first UK placement/import can change the position. Commercially sold used goods must still be safe, accurately described and of satisfactory quality taking account of their description, price and other circumstances.

Source: [Electrical Equipment (Safety) Regulations GB guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-equipment-safety-regulations-2016/electrical-equipment-safety-regulations-2016-great-britain) and [OPSS second-hand product guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/consumer-products-buying-second-hand-goods).

### Operational controls — mandatory/recommended

- Maintain build/component serials, suppliers, conformity documents, safety checks and customer traceability.
- Check recalls before sourcing used or auction components.
- Have a corrective-action/recall procedure and notify the relevant authority when required.
- Give English instructions and safety information; the planned build portal/manual library helps but should remain accessible after sale.
- Do not describe electrical testing as PAT certification if the actual process is different.

Source: [GOV.UK Product Safety Database guidance](https://report.product-safety-database.service.gov.uk/help/about).

## 8. WEEE, batteries and packaging

### WEEE — mandatory now

All electrical distributors must provide a route for customers to dispose of old like-for-like household electrical equipment. A retailer must provide a qualifying free takeback route or join the Distributor Takeback Scheme where eligible. Customers must receive free written information about the chosen service. Keep takeback/disposal and customer-information records for four years. Online-only retailers and sellers below £100,000 of annual EEE sales can be eligible for the DTS, subject to current scheme rules.

If FlipFlop manufactures and sells branded PCs, rebrands EEE or commercially imports it into the UK, it is also an EEE producer. Producers register annually; under 5 tonnes can register directly as a small producer, while above 5 tonnes requires a producer-compliance scheme. Producer marking, treatment information, registration-number and four-year record duties also apply.

Sources: [WEEE retailer responsibilities](https://www.gov.uk/electricalwaste-producer-supplier-responsibilities), [WEEE regulatory overview](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/regulations-waste-electrical-and-electronic-equipment), and [EEE producer responsibilities](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/electrical-and-electronic-equipment-eee-producer-responsibility).

### Batteries — conditional

The first UK person placing batteries, including batteries inside equipment, on the market is a battery producer and has registration/reporting duties. A retailer supplying at least 32kg of portable batteries per year must provide free takeback and publicise it, including on the website for an online seller. Below 32kg, retailer takeback is optional, although producer obligations may still apply if FlipFlop imports/first places batteries.

Sources: [battery producer responsibility](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/waste-batteries-producer-responsibility) and [battery retailer takeback](https://www.gov.uk/battery-waste-supplier-reponsibilities).

### Packaging EPR — conditional threshold

Every business should assess whether its packaging activities make it a producer. Registration/reporting starts where the business is established in the UK, supplies/imports more than 25 tonnes of packaging in the previous year and has worldwide annual turnover of at least £1 million. Small-producer duties apply above the defined thresholds; large producers have additional reporting/fee obligations. Monitor both turnover and packaging weight rather than assuming a sole trader is exempt.

Source: [EPR applicability guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-must-comply-with-epr-for-packaging--2) and [small-producer duties](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/epr-for-packaging-what-you-must-do-as-a-small-producer).

| Status | Position |
|---|---|
| **Not applicable at expected scale** | The statutory annual statement applies to qualifying commercial organisations carrying on business in the UK with annual turnover of at least £36 million, including relevant subsidiary turnover. |
| **Conditional** | If the threshold is reached, publish an annual statement and place a prominent homepage link. |
| **Recommended** | Maintain supplier due diligence and publish a narrowly worded voluntary responsible-sourcing statement if useful. Do not label it “required under section 54” unless the statutory test is met. |

## 10. Accessibility and equality

A private ecommerce business is not subject to the public-sector website accessibility regulations merely because it operates a website. It is, however, a service provider under Part 3 of the Equality Act 2010 and must not discriminate; the duty to make reasonable adjustments applies to disabled customers. There is no statutory requirement to publish an accessibility statement in the same form as a public body, but doing so is recommended.

Implement keyboard operation, meaningful labels and headings, visible focus, adequate contrast, text alternatives, captions/transcripts for tutorials, accessible authentication and error handling, and an accessible alternative for any 3D configurator or video-only guidance. State how customers can request help or an adjustment.

Source: [Equality Act 2010, Part 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/part/3).

## 11. Complaints and ADR

**Mandatory:** maintain a clear complaints contact and do not obscure statutory remedies. If FlipFlop is required by law or contract to use a particular ADR/complaints arrangement, tell a dissatisfied consumer about it when communicating the complaint outcome. Avoid saying that a customer must use ADR before enforcing legal rights unless that is genuinely lawful.

**Recommended:** publish a short complaints process with acknowledgement and response targets, escalation route, records and Trading Standards/Citizens Advice signposting. If voluntarily joining an ADR scheme, accurately name its status, scope and whether FlipFlop commits to use it.

Source: [Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, section 308 explanatory notes](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/notes/division/9/index.htm).

## 12. Finance, BNPL and insurance language

### Finance and BNPL — conditional

From 15 July 2026, third-party lender Deferred Payment Credit/BNPL is regulated where the statutory conditions are met. The lender must be authorised or hold the applicable permission. Merchant broking of qualifying DPC agreements has an exemption, but that does not permit FlipFlop to become the lender or make misleading promotions. Any website, social-media or product-page finance promotion must be fair, clear and not misleading, use lender-approved assets where required, explain material risks prominently and never imply that credit is guaranteed or irrelevant to the customer's circumstances.

Do not build an in-house instalment product without specific FCA perimeter advice.

Sources: [FCA BNPL regulation](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/regulating-buy-now-pay-later), [FCA financial promotions](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/financial-promotions-adverts), and [FCA CONC 3](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/CONC/3/).

### Delivery insurance — legal review

Buying parcel cover to protect FlipFlop's own transit risk is operational procurement. Selling, arranging or presenting an insurance product to the customer can cross into regulated insurance distribution. Because FlipFlop plans to include delivery protection in the product price, customer copy should describe safe insured delivery as FlipFlop's fulfilment commitment and make clear that FlipFlop remains the customer's contact for delivery problems. Do not describe the consumer as policyholder or sell an optional insurance add-on without FCA perimeter advice.

## 13. Recommended storefront page and checkout implementation

### Persistent footer

Show links to:

- Terms of Sale
- Delivery
- Returns & Cancellations
- Warranty & Repairs
- Privacy
- Cookies / Manage cookies
- WEEE & Recycling
- Accessibility
- Complaints
- Contact / legal identity

Footer identity example, replacing placeholders with real details:

> FlipFlop is a trading name of **[full legal name]**, a sole trader established at **[geographic address]**. Email: **[email]**. VAT number: **[only if registered]**.

### Checkout controls

- Show condition, core specification, selected options, warranty and delivery commitment.
- Show the final total, included compulsory costs and separately selected optional extras.
- Link the Terms, Delivery, Returns and Privacy pages before payment.
- Record the version of terms/policies accepted and email a durable copy.
- Use explicit, unticked early-performance/digital-content acknowledgements only when applicable.
- Offer a separate unticked marketing choice; a customer account/discount can be conditional on account creation, but the purchase must not force marketing consent.
- Label the final control **Place order and pay**.

### Marketplace fulfilment

- Preserve the listing and applicable terms at sale time.
- Send legally required durable information through a channel the marketplace permits.
- Keep the marketplace order ID, listing condition/specification, address snapshot and communications.
- Give indirect customers access to statutory remedies without requiring a marketing account.
- Use an in-box leaflet/QR claim flow only with secure verification; explain its privacy processing.

## 14. Matters requiring facts or professional review before publication

1. **Business identity/address:** insert the sole trader's legal name and a compliant geographic/service address. These cannot be invented from the repository.
2. **Custom-build cancellation:** classify Ready-to-ship, Curated-for-you, Made-by-you and Flip Refresh separately before invoking personalisation exclusions.
3. **Product conformity:** establish whether each new/custom PC makes FlipFlop the manufacturer; document applicable electrical, EMC, RoHS and radio rules and the NI sales position.
4. **WEEE/batteries:** confirm producer status from branding/import chains, annual EEE tonnage, battery weights and chosen distributor takeback route.
5. **AI processors:** document the actual models/providers, regions, processor terms, training/retention configuration and data fields sent.
6. **Delivery insurance:** keep it as trader-side risk cover unless FCA advice confirms a customer insurance arrangement.
7. **BNPL:** identify the provider and ensure all consumer-facing credit copy follows the provider's approved route and current FCA rules.
8. **Tax/VAT:** confirm VAT registration status and marketplace/international sales boundaries.
9. **Northern Ireland/international sales:** this report does not establish EU/NI or destination-country consumer/product compliance. Geo-limit sales until reviewed.

## 15. Compliance register to maintain

Maintain an internal register containing:

- proprietor/trading identity, addresses, VAT and ICO fee status;
- policy owner, version, effective date and linked checkout/email templates;
- cookie inventory and consent classification;
- data-processing inventory, vendors, contracts, transfers and retention periods;
- product compliance files, declarations, test/risk evidence, serials and recalls;
- WEEE producer/distributor registrations, takeback route and four-year records;
- battery and packaging threshold measurements;
- complaint, cancellation, repair, refund, warranty and safety-event records;
- BNPL-approved wording/assets; and
- quarterly review dates plus event-driven review when business model, vendor or law changes.

The chatbot should ingest published customer policies and customer-safe support material, but not use this research memo as the final legal wording. Answers about cancellation, faults, warranty and privacy should cite the controlling policy version and escalate disputes, safety issues, data-rights requests and insurance claims to a human.
